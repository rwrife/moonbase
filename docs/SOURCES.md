## User-reported historical outline

The 1990 game is described as a single-player lunar-base construction and
management game from Wesson International and KDT Industries, influenced by
NASA exploration studies. The outline reports oxygen, water, power, heat,
shelter, connected buildings, limited funding, exports, and accidents.
Reported buildings include habitats, landing pads, energy infrastructure,
mining, processing, oxygen production, labs, tourism facilities, and roads.

Review summaries differ in emphasis: export income, workers versus tourists,
markets, funding expectations, educational value, and limited late-game depth.
The design uses those themes as inspiration, not verified original mechanics.

## Explicit gaps

- The manual was not available to the owner and has not been verified here.
- Exact original costs, quantities, building inventory, controls, and formulas
  are unknown.
- A video titled "Nuclear Meltdown" is not sufficient evidence for a specific
  original failure probability or complete hazard model.
- The reported 1992/1993 Computer Gaming World quotations and recommendations
  need primary-source confirmation before being used in marketing.
- The supplied historical web pages were not independently fetched in this
  setup. Treat their contents as user-supplied context until verified.

## New design, not historical claims

Branching research, crew psychology, failure cascades, terrain resources,
landing windows, rival organizations, and a modern 3D camera are proposals for
this project. Prototype prices, day/night timing, output rates, and victory
conditions are new game rules. NASA-related provenance of the old game does not
imply NASA approval, endorsement, or scientific validation of this project.

## Legal and asset handling

Do not reuse the original title as a marketed game name without clearance.
Names and branding can raise trademark questions; art, audio, code, and manual
expression can raise copyright questions. Mechanics alone do not establish a
right to reuse protected expression. This is a practical project policy, not
legal advice. Choose an explicit project license and track asset provenance
before distributing builds or soliciting outside contributions.
